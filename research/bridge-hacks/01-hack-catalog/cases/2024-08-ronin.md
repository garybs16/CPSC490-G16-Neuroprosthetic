# Ronin Bridge, 2024-08-06

**Loss:** about $12M (about 3,996 ETH and about 2M USDC). Returned for a $500K bounty. · **Design:** lock-mint · **Root cause:** faulty upgrade/initialisation · **Chains:** Ethereum, Ronin · **Status:** confirmed

## Summary
A proxy upgrade left the bridge's operator-weight setting uninitialised, so withdrawal signatures were no longer checked properly. About 49 minutes later, MEV bots operating as whitehats withdrew the maximum that the per-transaction cap allowed. The bridge was paused 38 minutes after that. The cap kept the loss to about $12M; one analysis estimates it prevented about $72M more.

## Timeline (UTC)
| Time | Event |
|---|---|
| 08:48:47 | Proxy upgrade on bridge `0x64192819…af08` (block 20468438) |
| 09:37:23 | First withdrawal at the cap (block 20468679), about 49 minutes after the upgrade |
| 10:15:23 | Bridge paused (**38 minutes** after the first theft) |
| Following days | Funds returned in exchange for a $500K bounty |

## What failed
Faulty upgrade/initialisation: a new implementation was deployed without initialising a critical value.

## On-chain signature
- An `Upgraded` event on the bridge proxy.
- Within the hour, withdrawals that sat *exactly* at the per-transaction cap, to addresses with no deposit history.

## BridgeWatch rules that would fire
- `config_change`: on the upgrade, 49 minutes before any loss. This is the highest-value signal in this case.
- `large_withdrawal`: on the first capped withdrawal. A withdrawal exactly at a known limit is itself a useful flag.
- `outflow_spike`: on the same block.
- `escrow_drain` would probably **not** fire, because the cap limited the share drained.

## False-alarm considerations
- Upgrades are planned events. Routing `config_change` alerts to the team, with an "expected / unexpected" acknowledgement, keeps noise manageable.
- Large legitimate withdrawals near the cap do occur. The combination with a fresh upgrade is what makes this distinctive.

## Response and outcome
- Paused in 38 minutes.
- About 96% returned (minus the bounty).
- A strong case for hard rate limits as a partner control to detection.
