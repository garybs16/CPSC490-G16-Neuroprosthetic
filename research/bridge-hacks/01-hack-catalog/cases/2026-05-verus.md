# Verus-Ethereum bridge, 2026-05-17 and 2026-07-23

**Loss:** about $11.58M (May) and about $7.53M (July) · **Design:** lock-mint · **Root cause:** message/proof forgery (missing validation of the source-side amount) · **Chains:** Ethereum, Verus · **Status:** confirmed (both)

## Summary
In May, a crafted cross-chain payload passed the bridge's checks while committing almost no value on Verus. As a result, the Ethereum contract paid out 103.6 tBTC, 1,625 ETH and 147K USDC. The attacker returned about 75% for a bounty, and Verus put the returned funds back into the bridge on 8 July. On 23 July, a different attacker used the same contract, entry path and class of flaw to take about $7.5M more.

## What failed
Message/proof forgery: Ethereum-side payouts were not tied to value actually committed on Verus. The fix was not complete before liquidity returned.

## On-chain signature
- Multi-asset payouts from the bridge reserve to a fresh address.
- No matching lock on Verus.
- The second incident repeats the signature exactly.

## BridgeWatch rules that would fire
- `large_withdrawal` and `escrow_drain`: on the first payout block of each incident.
- `outflow_spike`: on the same block.
- `accounting_mismatch`: if Verus-side locks are reconciled.
- **Lesson for BridgeWatch:** after an incident, raise the vault's alert sensitivity until a post-mortem confirms the fix. The July drain hit a known-vulnerable contract 15 days after it was refilled.

## False-alarm considerations
- After a large redeposit (07-08), the baseline shifts. A detector re-learning from post-refill data must not treat a second drain as "returning to normal".

## Response and outcome
- May: about 75% returned.
- July: no recovery reported; funds swapped to about 3,916 ETH and sent to a mixer (CertiK via The Block).
