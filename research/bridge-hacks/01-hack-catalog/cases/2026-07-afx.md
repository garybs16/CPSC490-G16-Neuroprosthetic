# AFX Trade bridge (Arbitrum), 2026-07-22

**Loss:** about $24.15M USDC, nearly all of the vault's about $24.2M (DefiLlama via The Block) · **Design:** lock-mint (custom deposit bridge into the AFX L1) · **Root cause:** key/signer compromise. Only one outlet (CryptoBriefing via KuCoin) says this; AFX has not published a root cause. · **Chains:** Arbitrum, AFX · **Status:** confirmed

## Summary
The Arbitrum deposit contract of AFX, a derivatives L1, was emptied of almost all its USDC. A withdrawal request was made and then executed once a 200-second dispute window passed. The funds were bridged to Ethereum and swapped into about 12,467 ETH. AFX halted the bridge and offered a 30% bounty. Arbitrum's own canonical bridge was not involved.

## What failed
Unconfirmed. Reported as compromise of validator signing keys.

## On-chain signature
- A withdrawal *request* for nearly the full vault balance, visible about 200 seconds before the funds moved.
- Then one USDC transfer of about $24M to a fresh address.

## BridgeWatch rules that would fire
- `large_withdrawal`: on the **request** event, if pending withdrawals are monitored. That gives about 3 minutes of warning inside the dispute window.
- `escrow_drain` and `outflow_spike`: on execution.
- This is one of the few cases where a challenge window plus an alert could have stopped the loss. Arbitrum blocks are fast, so the detector must work by timestamp, not block count.

## False-alarm considerations
- Large market-maker withdrawals from a derivatives venue can be legitimate. A near-100% vault withdrawal request is not routine.

## Response and outcome
- Bridge halted; 70/30 settlement offered.
- No return reported at the time of writing.
