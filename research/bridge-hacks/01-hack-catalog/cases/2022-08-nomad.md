# Nomad Bridge, 2022-08-01

**Loss:** about $190M (rekt; DefiLlama; DeFiHackLabs says about $152M) · **Design:** optimistic messaging with lock-mint token router · **Root cause:** faulty upgrade/initialisation · **Chains:** Ethereum, Moonbeam, Evmos, Milkomeda · **Status:** confirmed

## Summary
An upgrade 41 days earlier initialised the bridge's trusted message root to a value that made unproven messages acceptable. Once the first exploiter showed this publicly, hundreds of copycat addresses repeated it. Nomad's Ethereum escrow was emptied in a chaotic free-for-all over a few hours.

## What failed
Faulty upgrade/initialisation: the root of trust was set to a default that every message matched.

## On-chain signature
- Hundreds of near-identical `process()` calls from more than 300 addresses, each releasing a fixed round amount.
- Many recipients were first-time users.
- The escrow balance fell step by step to near zero.

## BridgeWatch rules that would fire
- `withdrawal_burst`: in the first few blocks, because many releases came from many senders at once. This is the signature rule for this incident.
- `outflow_spike` and `escrow_drain`: within minutes, as cumulative outflow passed baseline.
- `config_change`: on the initialisation tx 41 days earlier. Without context it would most likely be read as a routine upgrade, but it gives a forensic anchor.

## False-alarm considerations
- Popular token launches or airdrop claims can produce bursts of similar withdrawals. Those come from wallets with prior deposits and are spread over many tokens and amounts. Identical round amounts from fresh addresses are unusual.

## Response and outcome
- Pausing the off-chain relayer did not help, because the flaw was in the contract.
- Nomad published a whitehat return address. Partial returns followed; the exact recovered share is an open question.
